Plastic Pollution Is Not Just an Ocean Problem

Plastic often starts on land, enters drains and rivers, and finally reaches lakes, coasts, and oceans—where it harms aquatic ecosystems and wildlife.

How Plastic Harms Aquatic Animals

Ingestion

Animals can mistake plastic for food, causing internal blockage, injury, reduced nutrition, starvation, and death.

Entanglement

Discarded nets, ropes, and lines can trap wildlife, restrict movement, and interfere with feeding and breathing.

Habitat Damage

Plastic debris and abandoned gear can physically damage reefs, wetlands, and benthic habitats.

Reduced Feeding / Nutrition

Plastic exposure can lower effective feeding, affecting growth and reproductive health.

Injury and Death

Sharp or persistent debris can cause wounds, infections, drowning risk, and mortality in turtles, fish, seabirds, and mammals.

Food-Web Transfer

Plastic particles can move from smaller organisms to higher trophic levels through aquatic food webs.

Evidence base: UNEP (2021), NOAA Marine Debris Program, peer-reviewed marine ecology studies listed in References.

Microplastics: The Invisible Threat

Large plastic items fragment over time into microplastics, which can be taken up by smaller organisms and move through aquatic food chains.

MICROPLASTICS ↓ PLANKTON / SMALL ORGANISMS ↓ FISH ↓ LARGER PREDATORS ↓ HUMANS

Research indicates food-web transfer is occurring, while full ecological and human-health implications are still under scientific investigation (WHO, UNEP, peer-reviewed literature).

Plastic Pollution in India

India’s river systems are critical to both ecosystems and communities. When waste management fails, plastic leakage into drains and waterways increases pressure on aquatic environments.

INDIA
River and drainage pathways matter.

Official India Plastic Waste (CPCB / GoI)

  • 2020–21: 4,126,997 tonnes
  • 2021–22: 3,901,802 tonnes
  • 2022–23: 4,136,188 tonnes
Source: PIB Press Release (2023) citing CPCB annual reporting; see references for links.

Government measures include Plastic Waste Management Rules, Extended Producer Responsibility (EPR), and actions on identified single-use plastic items.

What Can We Do?

1. REDUCE

Reduce unnecessary single-use plastic in daily consumption and packaging.

2. REUSE

Use durable alternatives and extend product life wherever possible.

3. RECYCLE

Strengthen segregation-at-source and recycling systems.

4. BETTER WASTE MANAGEMENT

Improve collection, sorting, transport, and safe disposal infrastructure.

5. PRODUCER RESPONSIBILITY

Apply EPR so producers, importers, and brand owners take responsibility for plastic packaging waste.

Policy note: India notified prohibition of identified single-use plastic items from 1 July 2022 under Plastic Waste Management amendments (MoEFCC/PIB/CPCB).
